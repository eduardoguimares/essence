# What is leadership

It's a reciprocal relationship between leader and follower

## Leadership as a construction

Leadership is not static, it evolves as society, organizations, and the challenges leaders face change. The concept of leadership has shifted from traditional command-and-control models to more collaborative, inclusive, and adaptive styles

For instance, the transformational leadership model, which focuses on inspiring and motivating followers to achieve higher levels of performance, is a relatively modern approach that reflects the changing nature of work and organizational goals

This construction is a dynamic process that requires continuous learning and a learning mindset. Leaders often learn best through experience. This involves a cycle of setting learning goals, experimenting with new approaches, and reflecting on the outcomes

The true test of a leader’s learning is in the application. Leaders must be able to apply what they’ve learned to new and challenging situations, continuously adapting and applying their knowledge to lead effectively

## Leader as a juggler

A leader needs to be able to manage multiple responsibilities simultaneously

## Leader as a gardener

A leader is someone who nurtures and cultivates growth. A leader fosters an environment where team members can develop their skills, innovate, and reach their full potential

## Leader as a conductor

A leader needs to be able to coordinate, balance perspectives and empower others in order to achieve collective success

# Common traits of effective leaders

- **Motivation to achieve**, driven, high level of energy, persistent in the right things, initiative to change things and make things happen
- **Motivation to lead**, willingness to assume responsibility and to seek power (socialized power motive - seeking power as a means to achieve desired goals, not personalized power motive - seeking power as an end in itself
- **Authentic**, they are honest, trustworthy, genuine, consistent, and promote trust and respect. They stay true to their values and maintain a high level of influence and engagement within their teams
	- That way they are capable of attracting and retaining followers
- **Charisma**, they inspire, energize, and motivate followers by creating a strong personal connection
	- They use powerful rhetoric and emotional appeals to inspire devotion and a shared vision
		- They use compelling narratives around their ideas or products
- **Emotional intelligence**, according to Daniel Goleman
- **Self-confidence**, ability to make hard decisions and stand on them, managing the perception of other on self-confidence and commanding their respect, emotionally stable
	- Needed to withstand setbacks, persevere through hard times and lead others in new directions
- **Cognitive abilities**, above average intelligence and capability of managing the perceptions of others on intelligence
	- In order to analyze situations accurately, solve problems effectively and make suitable decisions
	- But not necessarily genius, usually not
- **Knowledge of the business**, ability to gather and assimilate/adapt
	- Necessary for developing suitable visions, strategies and business plans
- **Courage** (not not being afraid, but having the capacity to do something even though you're afraid of it)

Leading by example

## Post-Heroic leadership

A concept that challenges traditional notions of leadership by emphasizing collaboration, teamwork, and shared accountability. It’s considered a newer-genre theory because it moves away from the classic image of a single, charismatic leader and instead focuses on the collective efforts of a group

Key characteristics:
- **Shared leadership** this dimension involves distributing leadership responsibilities among team members, allowing for multiple people to take on leadership roles depending on the task or project phase
- **Servant leadership** servant leaders prioritize the needs of their team members and help them develop and perform as highly as possible, rather than seeking personal glory.
- **Collective achievement** individual accomplishments are celebrated as part of the team’s overall success
- **Teamwork** open communication, mutual respect, and a shared vision
- **Shared accountability** accountability is not solely on the leader’s shoulders but is shared among all team members
- **Sharedness** leadership is a collective practice rather than a set of individual traits or behaviors
	- Leading as a practice leadership is seen as a social practice that occurs in the interactions between people, rather than as a characteristic of a particular individual
	- Positional leaders as tips of Icebergs positional leaders (like CEOs or managers) are visible symbols of leadership, but they are supported by a vast network of individuals who contribute to the leadership process
	- Importance of social networks and collaboration the effectiveness of leadership is increasingly dependent on the leader’s ability to navigate and leverage social networks and foster collaboration
- **Social process and interdependence** leadership occurs through relationships and interactions
	- Which is fomented by the need of human connections
	- Which means that leading and following involve understanding and responding to the dynamics of relationships
		- A mentor guiding a mentee relies on the ability to listen and provide feedback
		- The mentee’s role involves being open to learning and adapting
- **Leading change**
	- Collaboratively engaging all stakeholders in the change process
	- By leveraging shared practices
- **Culture of trust**
	- By applying feedback loops
	- By building trust within the teams

## Autocratic leadership

- **Makes decisions unilaterally**: decide on their own without much input from others
- **Maintains strict control** over organizational activities
- **Is authoritative**: provide explicit instructions and expect immediate compliance

## Democratic Leadership

- **Encourages collaboration and different perspectives**, involves team members in decision-making
- **Is Inclusive**
- **Promotes consensus building**: they facilitate discussions and aim for agreement
- **Empowers** others to contribute

## Transactional leadership

It is based on a relationship of mutual dependence and an exchange process of "I will give you this, if you do that"

It is based on legitimate authority within the bureaucratic structure of the organization

Gives emphasis on the clarification of the goals and objectives, work tasks and outcomes and organizational rewards and punishments

## Transformational leadership

It is based on the engendering higher levels of motivation and commitment among followers
- **Idealized influence**: the charisma of the leader, and the respect and admiration of the followers
- **Inspirational motivation**: the behavior of the leader, which provides meaning and challenge to the work of the follower by generating a vision for the organization and appealing to higher ideals and values than the self-interests of the individuals
	- That can create a feeling of justice, loyalty and trust
- **Intellectual stimulation**: leaders who solicit new and novel approaches for the performance of work and creative problem solutions from followers
- **Individualized consideration**: leaders who listen and give special concern to the growth and developmental needs of the followers

## Situational leadership

It's a more flexible form of leadership. A leader adapts their style to match the specific needs of a team or organization accordingly
- Directing/Telling style: When a team requires close supervision and guidance
	- High directive behaviour | Low supportive behaviour
	- Low confidence | High commitment
- Coaching/Selling style: When team members lack motivation or confidence
	- High directive behaviour | High supportive behaviour
	- Low to some confidence | Low commitment
- Supporting/Participating style: When the team is competent but lacks willingness or confidence
	- Low directive behaviour | High supportive behaviour
	- Moderate to high confidence | Varied commitment
- Delegating style: When a team is efficient and self-directed
	- Low directive behaviour | Low supportive behaviour
	- High confidence | High commitment

# [[Management]] in leadership

It refers to the ability of a leader to organize, plan and execute strategies that align with the organization's goals

## Managers vs leaders

Leaders have a different approach compared to managers
- Managers do things right while leaders do the right things
- Managers' relationships with others are based on authority and involve low levels of emotion while leaders' relationships are based on influence and empathy
- Managers focus on creating stability while leaders focus on creating change
- Managers adopt an impersonal or passive attitude toward goals while leaders adopt a more personal and active attitude toward goals
- Managers get people to accept solutions while leaders create excitement in work by developing choices that give substance to images that excite people
	- Managers needs to continually coordinate and balance in order to compromise conflicting values
- Managers are more conservators and regulators or the existing order while leaders' identity is not depended upon membership or work roles
	- Leaders work in but do not belong to the organization

Nonetheless, management has an important role in leadership

## Management skills in leadership
Effective leadership is not just about having a vision, it’s also about having the [[management]] skills to realize that vision. A leader with strong management skills can guide their team towards achieving goals while navigating the complexities of organizational dynamics

Skills like
- Strategic planning and decision-making
- Organizational skills - allow focus on strategic objectives
- Time management - ensures their teams are working on the right tasks at the right time
- Managing multiple responsibilities - Prioritizing tasks, delegating effectively and maintaining a balance between various projects and stakeholders’ needs
