System 1 and system 2 refer to two different modes of cognitive operation introduced by Daniel Kahneman[^1]. They are not identified in any specific part of the brain

The system 1 operates automatically and quickly, with little or no effort and no sense of voluntary control

The system 2 allocates attention to the effortful mental activities that demand it, including complex computations. These operations are often associated with the subjective experience of agency, choice and concentration. Using the system 2 requires much energy

The systems work with each other:
- The system 1 often gives impressions, intuitions, intentions and feelings to the system 2
- Sometimes the system 1 needs help from the system 2 to recognize illusions or to be able to deal with an unfamiliar situation
- When familiar situations arise, the system 2 will progressively let the control go to the system 1




[^1]: Title: *Thinking, fast and slow* 
Author: Daniel Kahneman
Date: 2011
ISBN: 978-0-374-27563-1
