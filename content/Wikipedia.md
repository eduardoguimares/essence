# Wikipedia's upsides

Some people approach Wikipedia as demonic website for the dissemination of pseudoscience and false information. While it's based on some truth, Wikipedia helps many people get the overall gist of a topic in a short amount of time

But more importantly, there are two main benefits of Wikipedia
- The articles' references tend to be a goldmine of useful sources if you want to delve deeply into a topic
- Contributing to Wikipedia contributes to you - try developing articles about niche topics you care about[^10] and you'll [[Learning|learn]] a lot

# Wikipedia's downsides

## [[Groupthink]]

Collective authorship tends to produce or align to mainstream or organizational beliefs and it creates a false sense of authority behind the information[^3] which reduces diversity and lowers standards[^9]

## Conflicts of interest

Nowadays there are companies that pay reputable wikipedians to alter articles in favor of their clients[^4]

Some people can edit a or their biography to hide or disguise some facts

## Left biases

Jweiss11 (top 100 Wikipedia contributor) claims that moderators tend to be left wing. That means that Wikipedia tends to be more lenient towards left ideas and more restrictive towards right ideas. Wikipedia lists some news sources (particularly some self-claimed left-wing) as reliable and other news sources (which tend to the right) not reliable[^2]

## Political content

Could be manipulated for political purposes

## Wikipedia is a Monopoly

Matthew White argued that that many niche websites from the 2000s are now gone because of the popularization of Wikipedia[^9]. Well, it's possible that people simply lost interest on those topics

## Sock puppets

Those are other accounts of the same person. There is no limit to the number of accounts you can create

The Wikipedia edits are associated with the IP address

Anyone can create other accounts to help the main account in a debate or to disrupt articles. When moderators find out, the IP address can be blocked indefinitely

## Long-term abuse individuals

There's a long list of individuals that continually disrupt articles, sometimes specific details, sometimes something else

# Wikipedia vs physical encyclopedias

Physical encyclopedias tend to get outdated where as Wikipedia tends to update fast. The writing of physical encyclopedias tends to be less accessible for the average user. Wikipedia is meant for the average person who wants to get the main point

However, there's also an argument that even encyclopedias have mistakes too, almost as much as Wikipedia (per article basis). *The average science entry in Wikipedia contained around four inaccuracies; Britannica, about three*[^5]

This is clearly a perspective-changing-piece-of-information as it suggests that the Wikipedia's biases or errors are *exception rather than the rule*[^5]

They claim that the survey was made by qualified individuals who chose to remain anonymous, but there was no sign of fact-checking their reviews. They seem to have approach their reviews as the correct fact. Some Britannica articles were merged for a better representation of the Britannica view of the topic

Britannica responded to the survey and claimed that Nature refused to provide the full reports on the encyclopedias errors. In fact, the document with the details of the reviews of the experts isn't the original and Britannica claims that they *exercised subjective judgment in redacting the original reports*[^6]

Britannica pointed out that the *organization of information, the quality of writing, and the readability of the article*[^6] are relevant characteristics of encyclopedias and pointed out all the errors made by the reviewers

Nature then responded with: *We see no reason to believe that any misidentifications of errors would adversely affect one publication more than the other*[^7]. They claim that if there were errors, there should errors at both sides
