*In its broadest sense, our understanding of workplace culture is that it embodies the ethos and values of a particular organization, which are expressed through the way it operates and the practices it undertakes. If the latter is deemed to be wrong, then the former needs to be addressed in order for any issues to be resolved effectively. Anything else would be superficial change and unlikely to have a lasting impact.*[^1]

Organization culture influences how employees interact, make decisions, and contribute to the overall success

# Functions of organizational culture

![[Organizational culture process|700]]

1. Identity expresses cultural understandings
2. Expressed identity leaves impressions on others that creates an image
3. Identity mirrors the images of others
4. That reflection embeds identity in the culture

## Identity and unity

Organizational culture provides a shared sense of identity and belonging among employees. It unites them around common values, beliefs, and norms

When employees identify with the organization’s culture, they feel connected and part of a larger whole. This sense of unity fosters teamwork, collaboration, and a collective commitment to organizational goals

> [!a] Example
> When Fernando Santos got the Portugal coach job in 2014, he communicated a very clear message: there's no "I", only a "We". That message was effective in making everyone in the team feeling they belong. That was specially important because at that time, there was a feeling that the team was only Cristiano Ronaldo.
> In the final of Fernando Santos first major competition with Portugal (Euro 2016), Cristiano Ronaldo got injured but they were able to win against France

## Social control

Organizational culture influences behavior and decision-making. It acts as an informal control mechanism, guiding employees’ actions and choices

Instead of relying solely on rigid rules and policies, culture shapes how employees interact, communicate, and make decisions. It encourages desirable behaviors and discourages undesirable ones

## [[Sensemaking]]

Organizational culture provides meaning and context for employees. It helps them understand why things happen, interpret events, and make sense of their work environment

When employees comprehend the underlying values and beliefs, they can align their actions with the organization’s purpose. This clarity enhances job satisfaction and engagement

# Perspectives of organizational culture

## Linda Smircich's cultural perspectives

### Culture as an independent variable

Culture is something an organization has and it is possible to categorize it

This is a mainstream perspective

### Culture as a metaphor

Culture is something an organization is. This metaphor contrasts with the 'organization is a machine' and 'organization is an organism' metaphors

Organization is the expression and manifestation of the human consciousness

*Organizations are understood and analyzed not mainly in economic or material terms, but in terms of their expressive, ideational, and symbolic aspects*[^2]

## Joanne Martin's culture perspectives

### Integration

Focused on the normalization and convergence of the individual's actions and behaviors towards a homogeneous and unique culture. Values, beliefs and norms are the same for every one. A stronger and united culture will mean better performance

### Differentiation

There are subcultures usually in big organizations where there's a lot of variety in professional roles, ages and geographical divisions. Subcultures sometimes overlap and conflict with each other

### Fragmentation

Culture and subculture are too superficial to understand reality, therefore it is necessary to look into each individual's own agenda, behaviors and way of thinking. The inexistence of consensus is the norm but the groups are reconciled through management
