[[Organization]]s are not just about tasks and processes. They are also arenas where power dynamics play out

Recognizing the political dimension helps us navigate organizational complexities

# Sources of power struggles

- Resource scarcity (budget, personnel, time, etc.)
- Competing goals and priorities
- Role ambiguity and overlaps
- Information asymmetry - unequal access to information
- Organizational change - during restructuring or leadership transitions, power dynamics shift
- Personal agendas and egos

# Perspectives

## Unitarist perspective

The unitarist perspective is a theoretical framework in organizational behavior and management. It assumes that everyone within an organization shares common goals and interests. In other words, it views the organization as a harmonious entity where employees and management work together towards a unified purpose
- Shared objectives - all employees, regardless of their roles, have a common purpose. They align their efforts with the overall goals of the organization
- Harmony - emphasis on cooperation, collaboration and a lack of conflict within the organization. It assumes that conflicts are rare and can be resolved through communication and understanding
- Single vision - organizations have a single vision that guides decision-making and actions. Employees are expected to contribute to achieving this vision

## Pluralist perspective

It acknowledges that within an organization or society, various stakeholders have different interests, needs, and priorities. These diverse interests can include employees, management, customers, unions, and other groups

Negotiation is an important component to manage conflicting interests. It involves discussions, bargaining and reaching agreements to address divergent viewpoints

## Structuralist perspective

The structuralist perspective posits that power is intricately woven into the fabric of formal organizational structures, hierarchies, and design. It suggests that individuals’ authority and influence are shaped by their positions within these structures

### Post-structuralist perspective

The post-structuralist perspective challenges the notion of fixed power structures. It seeks to unravel and question the underlying dynamics that shape authority, influence, and control
