Ambidexterity refers to an organizational approach where the organization finds the right balance between exploration and exploitation, aiming to achieve both capabilities simultaneously at the organizational level

**Exploration** or [[Innovation]] - seeking new opportunities, experimenting and venturing into uncharted territory. Focuses on the helpful and external factors (Opportunities) in the [[SWOT analysis]]

**Exploitation** focus on optimizing existing resources, processes and capabilities to maximize efficiency and productivity. Focuses on the harmful and internal factors (Weaknesses) in the [[SWOT analysis]]

# Structural ambidexterity

Separated units for alignment and for adaptability which operate independently. The top of the organization decides the split between alignment and adaptability, defines the structure and makes trade-offs. Roles are clearly defined. Employees are specialists

## Critical perspective

Separated units can lead them not being well connected and hinder collaboration, affecting negatively the organizational performance. It can hinder resource-constrained organizations due to inefficient resource allocation. It can create resistance to change due to entrenched cultural norms

The [[Leadership]] style and organizational culture can conflict with the efficiency and innovation-driven approach

The hierarchical structure can hinder exploration efforts. That's why it's necessary to align structural ambidexterity with organizational context

# Contextual ambidexterity

Each employee (the front line) divides their time between activities that focus on alignment and activities that focus on adaptability. The top of the organization develops the organizational context in which individuals act. Roles are flexible. Employees are generalists
