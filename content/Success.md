# What is success?

Success nowadays is typically associated with making a lot of money because money can get you many things. Other times it is associated with a concrete achievement like prizes and such

But success could be more abstract like developing a family, support altruistic causes or simply finding stability and well-being in life

Another way to [[perspective]] success is to add the [[Perspective#Time axis|time axis]]. Instead of attributing success to a timely state, you could attribute success to the degree of growth and approximation to the desire state. That requires a vision and, therefore, a purpose

# Basic principles

## Persistence

*If there is no struggle, there is no progress*[^1]

*Ninety percent of success can be boiled down to consistently doing the obvious thing for an uncommonly long period of time without convincing yourself that you're smarter than you are*[^2]

*Don’t aim at success—the more you aim at it and make it a target, the more you are going to miss it. For success, like happiness, cannot be pursued; it must ensue, and it only does so as the unintended side-effect of one’s dedication…In the long run—in the long run, I say!—success will follow you precisely because you had forgotten to think about it*[^4]

### The dip[^6]

The process of development very few people are willing to get through. If you find a market niche with potential and go through the dip, you will become a rare and precious element in the job market

In order to get through the dip you'll need to be willing to sacrifice other activities and focus most of your effort in one segment. It needs to be challenging. Probably not in the beginning because it is new, but you would need to go through a period of deep focus and effort that doesn't seem to have progress

*"A few people will choose to do the brave thing and end up the best in the world. Informed people will probably choose to do the mature thing and save their resources for a project they're truly passionate about."*

Quit if you reach the cul-de-sac - it's boring and there's no progression

Quit if you reach cliff - it's exciting for a while but it leads to failure

### How?

Find the pain you're most comfortable with. Which pain you're most willing to have? Because [[Suffering|pain]] is inevitable and part of growth

The more knowledgeable or better you get at something, the more you'll enjoy it

## Sacrifices/Prioritization

We need to know when and how to quit[^6] for better energy allocation

*If you really want to be great at something, you have to truly care about it. If you want to be great in a particular area, you have to obsess over it. A lot of people say they want to be great, but they're not willing to make the sacrifices necessary to achieve greatness. They have other concerns, and they spread themselves out. ... Greatness isn't easy to achieve. It requires a lot of time, a lot of sacrifices. It requires a lot of tough choices. It requires your loved ones to sacrifice, too. So you have to have an understanding circle of family and friends. People don't always understand just how much effort from how many people goes into one person chasing a dream to be great. There's a fine balance between obsessing about your craft and being there for your family. It's akin to walking a tightrope. Your legs are shaky, and you're trying to find your center. Whenever you lean too far in one direction, you correct your course and end up over leaning in the other direction. So you correct by leaning the other way again. That's the dance. You can't achieve greatness by walking a straight line*[^5]

# Is success skill or luck?

## If it is mainly luck

Be aware of Survivorship [[Bias]]

## If it is both

Successful people might have trouble acknowledge that luck played a role in their success because they remember more vividly everything they have done and easily forget everything out of their control that had an indirect impact on their success (luck). This is called Egocentric [[bias]]

However, the fact that luck has a very important role in success doesn't mean that hard work isn't has impactful, like 90% luck and 10% hard work. It just means that of the people that are very hard working, they still need to be very lucky to achieve a high level of success[^3]

> [!a] Example
> Let's say that 1000 people apply to NASA but only 10 are selected. Also, the criteria for the selection is hard work (95%) and luck (5%). Everyone's scores were randomized. The simulation was done 1000 times and it concluded that in order to be selected you had to have on average 94% in luck and also to be very hard working. Even though luck only accounted for 5%, it is still very impactful. If luck was not involved at all 8 out of 10 participants on average would be different

## If it is mainly hard work

Even if luck is the most significant factor for the success considering just one opportunity, having multiple opportunities in life allows hard-work to be the main factor for success
