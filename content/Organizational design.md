Organizational design aims to align the shape and structure of an organization with its strategic goals and objectives

It involves reviewing what the organization wants and needs, analyzing the gap between its current state and desired state, and designing practices that bridge that gap

# What to influence

## "Hardware"

Structural elements the formal arrangement of roles, reporting lines and departments

Roles and responsibilities what each employee does and how their work contributes to overall goals

Enabling mechanisms decision-making processes, performance management and talent management

## "Software"

Values, culture, leadership, individual talent (recognizing the skills, capabilities and potential of employees)

## [[Ambidexterity]]

# Fayol bureaucracy design

Henri Fayol developed 14 principles of management
1. Division of Work
	- Work should be divided based on individual's expertise
	- Specialization improves efficiency
2. Authority and Responsibility - managers have the authority to make sure their instructions are executed and they have accountability
3. Discipline
	- Employees must follow rules
	- Essential for systematic functioning
4. Unity of Command - each employee reports to one supervisor
5. Unity of Direction - common objectives for the organization
6. Subordination of Individual Interests - organizational goals come first
7. Remuneration - fair compensation motivates employees
8. Centralization - balance between centralization and decentralization
9. Scalar chain - hierarchical communication flow
10. Order - organized workplace
11. Equity - fair treatment for all
12. Stability of Tenure - retain skilled employees
13. Initiative - encourage innovation
14. Esprit de Corps - team spirit and unity

# Max Weber bureaucracy theory

1. Formal Hierarchy clear chain of command
2. Division of Labor specialization
3. Rules and Procedures standardized processes
4. Impersonality decisions are based on rules, not personal preferences
5. Career-Based Employment merit-based promotions
6. Efficiency and Rationality systematic approach

## Criticisms

Rigidity - Lack of flexibility

Red Tape - Excessive rules

Dehumanization - Focus on rules over people

# Burns and Stalker contingency theory

In the 1960s, Burns and Stalker proposed that organizational structures should adapt to the external environment. Organic approach in contrast with a mechanistic approach

| Organic approach                                                                                          | Mechanistic approach                                                                |
| --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Flexibility adapting quickly to the changing market conditions                                            | Stability adherence to the proven methods and processes for productions             |
| Decentralization of the decision-making processes authority is distributed                                | Centralization of the decision-making processes authority rests with top management |
| Fluid communication communication channels are open, and information flows freely among team members      | Formal communication communication flows and depends on the hierarchical channels   |
| Dynamic roles employees collaborate across functions                                                      | Specialized roles                                                                   |
| Flat hierarchy few layers of management exist, promoting direct interaction between leaders and employees | Tall hierarchy multiple layers of management with clear reporting lines             |

# Mintzberg managerial roles and organizational configurations

1970s
- Simple structure - an unique central leader makes decisions
- Machine bureaucracy - hierarchical, rule-driven organizations (factories)
- Professional bureaucracy - expertise-based (universities, law firms, accountant firms)
- Divisionalized - form divisions operate independently
- Adhocracy - flexible and project-based (startups, creative agencies)

# Business Process Reengineering

Early 1990s

Process redesign - radical process changes to improve efficiency, quality and, consequently, customer satisfaction

It aims for a complete overhaul, not incremental improvements. It analyses the complete process from beginning to end. It challenges existing practices and assumptions

Process
1. Identify and understand the current process
2. Review the current process
3. Design process improvements
4. Implement improvements

# Total Quality Management

1920s and 1930s

Emphasis on continuous improvement and excellence in all aspects of the organization -> [[Mentality#Kaizen philosophy|Kaizen philosophy]]
- Employees are encouraged to provide feedback, suggest process enhancements and participate in quality circles
	- "Lunch-and-learn" sessions allow employees to share best practices and innovative ideas
- Customer satisfaction
	- Human resource management identifies critical skills needed by employees to meet customer expectations and creates personalized **training programs**
- Total employee involvement
	- Human resource management encourages **all employees** to actively participate in quality improvement initiatives
		- Cross-functional teams are formed to identify process bottlenecks, suggest improvements and implement changes
- Human resource management plays a crucial role
	- Human resource management identifies critical skills needed by employees to meet customer expectations and creates personalized **training programs**
	- Human resource management encourages **all employees** to actively participate in quality improvement initiatives
		- Cross-functional teams are formed to identify process bottlenecks, suggest improvements and implement changes
	- Human resource management collaborates with **process** owners to map out key processes
	- Data guides human resource management
		- They collect data related to employee performance, training effectiveness and satisfaction
		- They do regular surveys to assess employee morale, job satisfaction and perceptions or quality-related practices

## Critical perspective

Rigid implementation of TQM can stifle innovation and adaptability. Organizations often struggle to balance the pursuit of quality with the need for agility and responsiveness to changing market demands, highlighting the tension between TQM principles and structural ambidexterity
