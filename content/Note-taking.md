# Why

- To make our thoughts clear - helps [[thinking]]
	- *While we naturally understand that writing is a good way to share ideas with others, we under-appreciate just how much good writing helps us think about an idea ourselves. Writing is not only a means of communication, it enables us to practice reasoning*[^4]
	- *Good [[thinking]] is expensive. Bad thinking costs a fortune. One way to force yourself to think is to write. **Good writing requires good thinking**. Forcing yourself to make your thinking visible gives poor thinking nowhere to hide. You can’t simply take a few minutes here and there, get the gist of the problem, and expect to have clear writing. It doesn’t work that way. Good writing, like good thinking, takes time*[^5]
- To Journal - contributes to mindfulness
- To store [[knowledge]] and [[information]] - frees our brain for other mental processes, like having ideas[^1]
	- Short-term - increasing your capacity to hold on the information necessary for the execution of current tasks
	- Long-term - cataloging like a library

# The 4 types of note takers

There are 4 main type of note takers[^1]

- The Architect - System oriented
- The Gardener - Idea oriented
- The Librarian - Knowledge oriented
- The Student - Learning oriented (a mix of the previous types)

## Which one to choose

Tiago Forte recommends choosing a note-taking app that is compatible with our note-taking approach[^1]. In my opinion, the best note-taking app is the one that allows us to better reach our goals. We might have different goals that require different types of note-taking apps

# Methods of note-taking

## By using the C.O.D.E. method[^1]

1. Capture
2. Organize
3. Distill
4. Express

## Out-based vs in-based

Out-based - taking notes based on the sources. For example, a note for a summary of a book or a note for an university lecture. The notes will end up organized based on the structure of your sources

In-based - taking notes based on your own ideas. That way, you create a note per idea and add whatever is related with the topic in the note[^3]

## Zettelksaten method

Combines the out-based and in-based approaches

1. Find ideas
2. Take notes of ideas that pop into your head and content you read/hear so you don't forget - these are fleeting notes
3. Review your previous notes and store in a permanent note-taking vault
	- One idea per note
	- Make connections between notes
4. Organize your permanent notes into a slip box
	- Number each note
	- If a thought is built off of a previous thought, file the note behind the original
