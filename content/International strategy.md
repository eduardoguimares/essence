**International [[strategy]]** refers to a set of options for an organization to operate outside its country of origin

**Global strategy** is a type of international strategy that involves coordinating a high number of activities dispersed in many countries of the world. Global sourcing refers to the purchasing of products or services to the most adequate suppliers regardless of their country

# Internationalization factors

## Market factors

- Similar needs from clients around the world
- Marketing that can be used worldwide

## Cost factors

- Economies of scale beyond local market
- Differences in the job market costs
	- Cheaper labor in Africa and Asia
 - Lower tax costs
- Transportation costs that are not much higher
	- Computer chips transportation costs may be worth producing overseas but furniture transportation costs would be too expensive

## Governmental factors

- Commercial policies
	- WTO commercial policies
	- Less or no commercial barriers between countries in the EU
- Adoption of free markets
- Less bureaucracy
- Technical standardization across every country

## Competitive factors

- Interdependence between countries
- Investment incentives - some countries offer investment incentives in specific industries
- [[Strategic competences]]
	- Universities
- Reputation
	- Belgian chocolate
- Competitors strategy - companies not following this strategy might have more difficulty competing

# Localization factors

Porter's diamond model can helps us understand why in some locations organizations have more sustainable competitive advantages than in other locations

![[Porter's diamond model|700]]

**Factor conditions**
- Natural factor - language in the case of the banking industry in Switzerland
- Created factor - price of energy in the aluminium industry

**Demand conditions**
- Exigence - fashion in France and Italy
- Dimension - truck engines in the USA

**Related and supported industries**
- Silicon Valley for hardware, software, research and venture capital
- North of Italy for the shoe industry, machinery, design, etc.

**Strategy, structure and rivalry**
- Japan's car industry

Additionally **governmental policies** can help increase these advantages by
- increasing environmental and security standards
- encouraging suppliers and buyers cooperation at a local level by creating clusters

# International strategies

![[International strategies|700]]

**Exportation strategy** - leverages the products of the country of origin
- Works with strong brands

**Multidomestic strategy** - emphasis on the local by providing different offers for different countries
- More common with organizations oriented to the market (Food industry)
	- Unilever ice cream brands in different countries
		- Olá
		- Wall's
		- Lusso
		- HB
		- Miko
		- ...
	- Jerónimo Martins' supermarkets
		- Portugal - Pingo Doce
		- Colombia - Ara
		- Poland - Biedronka
- Provides more autonomy for managers and each local brand has its own value chains
- But there are inefficiencies, variations in quality and brand value might get diluted

**Global strategy** - activities are geographically disperse but controlled centrally and the global market is approached as one
- Better integration and efficiency
- Products and services are standardized in order to serve all markets and reach economies of scale
- More appropriate for companies that produce commodities and products that satisfy common needs across the world
- But low flexibility in the adaption of products and services to the local needs

**Transnational strategy** - tries to get the best from the global integration and local response, while minimizing weaknesses
- It tries to maximize the sharing of knowledge across disperse units
- Achieves operational efficiency but products and services are still adapted to the local conditions
- But it might difficult to implement since it's very complex
