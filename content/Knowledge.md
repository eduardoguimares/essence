# Explicit and tacit knowledge

Explicit knowledge is formalized and codified

Tacit knowledge is difficult to express or extract because it is rooted in action and context. Therefore it is more difficult to transfer to others by means of writing it down or verbalizing it. This can include motor skills, personal wisdom, experience, insight and intuition. This is the type of knowledge [[Leadership|Leader]]s should be able to communicate clearly

# Knowledge management in organizations

It involves identifying, organizing, storing, and disseminating information

Knowledge management enables organizations to systematically create, share, and apply knowledge, improving capabilities and achieving strategic objectives efficiently. When knowledge is not easily accessible, it can be costly in terms of time spent seeking relevant information instead of focusing on outcome-driven tasks

It involves explicit, tacit and implicit knowledge (not yet documented but exists within processes (often referred to as "know-how"))

Organizations use [[Enterprise systems#Knowledge management system|knowledge management systems]]

### Communities of practice

Groups of individuals with shared interests or expertise. They collaborate, exchange knowledge, and solve problems

> [!a] For example
Developer communities
Project teams
Industry-specific groups

### Knowledge intake

**Tutoring and training** - Knowledge transfer through training sessions, workshops and mentorship programs. Important for an adequate onboarding process

**Q&A platforms** - Allows employees to ask questions, seek answers, and share knowledge - These platforms encourage active participation and foster a culture of continuous learning

**Notebooks and files**

**Customer queries and complaints** - Interactions with customers provide tacit knowledge about their needs and pain points
