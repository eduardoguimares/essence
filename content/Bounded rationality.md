Bounded rationality is a term introduced by Herbert Simon to describe the cognitive limitations of the human [[Rationality]], in contrast with the [[Homo economicus]] narrative[^1]. That considered, Herbert Simon proposes the [[Satisficing]] decision-making strategy instead of an optimal decision-making strategy, which is humanly impossible

# Limitations[^2]

- Limited knowledge
- Cognitive capacity
- Time limits

[[Decision making|Decisions]] are limited because decision-makers don't have the full information neither have the mental capacity to make the perfect decision

# Effect in organizations

The bounded rationality concept explains why two members from different departments make different decisions while members from the same department tend to make the same decision. Their rationality is bound in different ways due to the different set of goals each of them are subject to[^3]
