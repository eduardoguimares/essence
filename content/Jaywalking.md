Jay = rube, meaning they don't belong to the city or they don't know how to behave there

The term 'Jay' was first used by Kansas City Star in 1905 to criticize reckless drivers. In the same year, the term was used against pedestrians as 'jaywalkers'

In the early 20th century, when people started using cars in the streets and accidents started to increase, a petition was created to limit the maximum car speed. The car industry retaliated by creating the 'Model Municipal Traffic Ordinance' meant to regulate instead the pedestrians, by teaching people across the USA the new term 'jaywalking' and teaching them about the pedestrian rules. *Those who didn't abide by the new rules were publicly called out*[^1], they were ridiculed even by judge impersonators. *Developing a culture of shame was the most important tool in reframing street safety as the responsibility of the pedestrian*[^1]


[^1]: *Jaywalking: How It Became Illegal To Cross The Street*, Cheddar (2022)
