# Follow your passion?

*Just because you have a passion for something doesn't mean you don't suck at it*[^2]

## As a student

Following a passion when you are a student is very different from following a passion when you are an adult[^1]. In the beginning you're trying to add value to yourself and you are financially protected. But if you're trying to make your passion as a career, then you'd need to understand how to add value to others. An entrepreneur might try to understand costumer needs. A musician might try to connect artistically to others

It's ok to not have a passion. In that case the approach would be to just seek new opportunities and to find what you enjoy[^1]

# Specialization or generalization

David Epstein argues that late specializers in education systems tend to have better job matches and income growth over time[^7]

## A different [[perspective]]

Nowadays, specialization is done by area of field ([[Extroverted thinking|Te]] driven) but specialization could also be done by area of impact ([[Introverted intuition|Ni]] driven): focus on a goal and learning the areas of field necessary to achieve that goal

## Business-wise

In a business point of view, specialization on a [[Market segments|market segment]] is regarded as essential to survive the company's birth and start its orbit in the business world. Then, the usual [[strategy]] to grow is to generalize its products and business units

# Find opportunities

Your added value should be in line with the external environment[^1]. What's possible now that wasn't possible 10 years ago? What market will be very saturated 10 years from now?

> [!a] Examples
> Focusing on Youtube in the early 2010s
> Focusing on data analytics in the early 2000s
